# Discipline Branches

Each branch gives you two things: prompts for the evidence half (Step 3) and gap options for the
identification step (Step 5). Follow-ups in Step 6 should break each selected gap into three or
four more specific flavors, keeping the multi-select plus free-text pattern.

See `product-branch.md` for a fully worked example of how deep a branch goes when there's real
calibration behind it.

**Honesty note.** The Product branch is calibrated from real interview and assignment feedback.
Design, Engineering, Sales, and Support are reasonable starting points based on general knowledge
of what each discipline is evaluated on — they are *not* calibrated the same way. Treat them as
something for the person to react to and correct via free text, not as an authoritative
diagnostic. Say so plainly if asked.

## DESIGN

**Step 2 context:** in-house / agency / freelance; product design / brand / systems.

**Step 3 evidence prompts:**
- Domains: which product surfaces or industries you've designed for long enough to know the
  conventions and when to break them
- Shipped: flows that went live and what changed in the numbers after
- Unfair advantage: front-end code, research training, a specialty like accessibility or motion

**Step 5 gap options:**
- Solutions feel polished but aren't grounded in user research or insight
- Doesn't articulate the "why" behind design decisions — rationale isn't defensible
- Misses systemic thinking: design system, scalability, edge and empty states
- Doesn't account for engineering feasibility or handoff
- Something else — I'll describe it

## ENGINEERING

**Step 2 context:** backend / frontend / full-stack / infra / ML; IC or tech lead track.

**Step 3 evidence prompts:**
- Domains: systems, languages, or problem spaces you've gone deep enough on to know the failure
  modes
- Shipped: what you built, what traffic or scale it handled, what broke and what you did
- Unfair advantage: production ownership, on-call scar tissue, a specialty like performance or
  security

**Step 5 gap options:**
- Solution works but isn't scalable or maintainable long-term
- Doesn't surface trade-offs (performance vs. delivery speed, build vs. buy)
- Misses edge cases and failure modes
- Communicates in implementation details rather than problem and impact
- Something else — I'll describe it

## SALES

**Step 2 context:** SMB / mid-market / enterprise; new business / expansion; deal sizes and cycle
length.

**Step 3 evidence prompts:**
- Domains: industries and buyer personas you've sold into repeatedly
- Shipped: quota attainment, largest deal, a deal you won that shouldn't have been winnable
- Unfair advantage: a prior career in the industry you sell to, technical depth, a network

**Step 5 gap options:**
- Pitches features rather than business outcomes and ROI
- Doesn't handle objections — moves past them instead of resolving them
- Misses the economic buyer or the full decision-making unit
- Lacks urgency or a clear next step in the close
- Something else — I'll describe it

---

## SUPPORT / CUSTOMER SUCCESS

**Step 2 context:** support / CS / technical account management; SMB volume or enterprise
named-account.

**Step 3 evidence prompts:**
- Domains: products or technical areas you can debug without escalating
- Shipped: retention or CSAT numbers you moved, a churn you reversed, a process you built
- Unfair advantage: engineering ability, a vertical specialty, a language

**Step 5 gap options:**
- Resolves the immediate ticket but doesn't address root cause
- Doesn't tie individual issues back to product or process improvement
- Reactive rather than proactive — doesn't flag risk before it becomes churn
- Struggles to push back on unreasonable asks, internal or customer-facing
- Something else — I'll describe it

## Universal free-text follow-up

Use this for any gap where you don't have discipline-specific texture to follow up with:

> "What did that look like the last time it happened? What was the actual feedback, in their
> words if you remember them?"
